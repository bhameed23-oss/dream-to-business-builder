# Auditing Phase Resources

## Self-assessment tools

- **[SBA - Business Guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide)** — Free step-by-step guides broken out by business stage, useful for comparing where you are against where you "should" be.
- **[SCORE - Business Health Assessments](https://www.score.org)** — Free mentors can walk through an outside perspective on your operations, which is often more honest than a self-audit.

## Security and privacy basics

- **[FTC - Cybersecurity for Small Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/small-businesses/cybersecurity)** — Free, plain-language basics on protecting customer data.
- **[CISA Small Business Resources](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/cyber-essentials-toolkits)** — Free federal resources on basic cybersecurity practices.

## Legal and risk review

- **Your state bar association's lawyer referral service** — Most states have a low-cost referral service to find a business attorney for a one-time consultation.
- **[SBA - Insurance guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/get-business-insurance)** — Overview of what kinds of business insurance exist and when you need them.

## When to bring in a professional

Talk to a lawyer, accountant, or security consultant if your audit turns up:
- Customer data you're not sure is being stored safely
- Contracts or agreements you never had reviewed
- Anything that feels like it "probably should have been handled differently a while ago"

That feeling is exactly what the auditing phase is meant to surface — it's a good sign the process is working, not a reason to panic.
