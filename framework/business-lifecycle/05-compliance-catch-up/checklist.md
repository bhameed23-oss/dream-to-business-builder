# Compliance Catch-Up Checklist

Use this after reading README.md in this folder. This phase is about finding and fixing legal, privacy, and risk gaps — calmly and in order, not all at once.

**Reminder: this checklist helps you find and organize gaps. It does not replace a lawyer or compliance professional for actually closing them.**

## 1. Find out what data you're actually holding

- [ ] List every place customer information lives (spreadsheets, a CRM, email, paper files, payment processor).
- [ ] For each one, note: what type of data is it (name, email, phone, payment info, health info, etc.)?

## 2. Check how it's protected

- [ ] Who has access to each of those places? Is access limited to people who actually need it?
- [ ] Is any of it password-protected, encrypted, or otherwise secured — or just sitting open?

## 3. Review money and payments

- [ ] How do you collect payment? Is it through a recognized, secure processor (Stripe, Square, PayPal, etc.) rather than something informal?
- [ ] Do you store any card numbers yourself? (If yes, flag this immediately — this is a serious compliance gap in most cases.)

## 4. Review communications and marketing

- [ ] If you email or text customers, did they actually opt in? Do you have a way to prove it?
- [ ] Is there an easy way for someone to unsubscribe or opt out?

## 5. Review documentation and policies

- [ ] Do you have a privacy policy if you collect any customer data online?
- [ ] Do you have contracts or terms of service for what you sell, if applicable?

## 6. Build your remediation list

- [ ] List every gap you found, ranked by risk (data exposure and payment issues are usually highest priority).
- [ ] Mark which ones need a professional (lawyer, accountant, security consultant) versus which ones you can fix yourself today.

## Done when

You know exactly what data you hold and where, you've flagged anything risky about how it's handled, and you have a ranked list of what to fix — with the highest-risk items marked for professional review.
