# Compliance Catch-Up Resources

**None of this is legal advice. These are starting points to understand the landscape before you talk to a professional.**

## Privacy and data

- **[FTC - Protecting Consumer Privacy](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security)** — Federal Trade Commission's plain-language guidance on business data privacy obligations.
- **[FTC - Children's Privacy (COPPA)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy)** — Important if your customers could include anyone under 13.
- **State privacy laws** — California (CCPA), Virginia, Colorado, and other states have their own rules. Search "[your state] consumer privacy law small business" to check if you're covered.

## Payments

- **[PCI Security Standards Council](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/)** — The real standard for handling card payments safely. If you're not using a processor like Stripe or Square, you likely need to understand this.

## Marketing and communication compliance

- **[FTC - CAN-SPAM Act guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)** — Rules for business emails.
- **[FCC - TCPA basics](https://www.fcc.gov/general/telemarketing-and-robocalls)** — Rules for text messages and calls to customers.

## Finding a professional

- **Your state bar association's lawyer referral service** — Low-cost initial consultations for business/privacy law questions.
- **A local CPA (certified public accountant)** — For payment, tax, and financial compliance questions specifically.

## When to stop and get help immediately

- You store customer card numbers directly (not through a processor)
- You collect health information and aren't sure if HIPAA applies to you
- You're not sure whether a law applies to you at all — that uncertainty itself is worth a one-time consultation to resolve
