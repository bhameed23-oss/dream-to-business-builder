# Assessment Framework

This phase helps the user understand what kind of business they are actually building and what they need before they choose tools, branding, automation, or launch strategy.

## Purpose

The assessment phase answers questions like:

- What does the business actually do?
- Who does it serve?
- What problem does it solve?
- What is the core promise?
- What is happening manually today?
- What is currently broken or inefficient?
- What is missing in the business system?
- What needs to be handled with human review or professional support?

## Core output

By the end of this phase, the user should have:

- a clear business summary
- customer profile
- service or product summary
- operations summary
- current process map
- major pain points
- top opportunities
- risk summary
- next-step recommendations

## Assessment questions

### Business identity
- What is the business name or proposed name?
- Is the business new or existing?
- What does the business sell or do?
- Who is the ideal customer?
- What problem is being solved?

### Customer and market
- Who buys this service product today?
- Why do they choose this business?
- What does the customer experience currently look like?
- What kind of repeat business or referrals matter most?

### Operations and workflow
- What is done manually?
- What is done poorly or inconsistently?
- Where are bottlenecks?
- What would automate the most value quickly?

### Risks
- What information do you collect from customers or staff?
- What might go wrong if the system breaks?
- Are you handling payments, sensitive data, or records?
- Do you need legal or compliance review?

## Suggested process

1. Ask the user essential questions
2. Summarize business context
3. Capture operational pain points
4. identify missing systems and workflow gaps
5. identify compliance and professional review triggers
6. present 3 option paths
7. recommend one path
8. require approval before proceeding

## Guardrails

Do not push a user into automation too early. The framework should help them understand the business before designing tools or systems.

If the user is handling customer data, regulated processes, legal issues, payment flows, or personally identifiable information, add a review flag and pause before proceeding.

## Example outputs

- business summary
- service summary
- customer summary
- operations summary
- automation target list
- risk flag list
- next milestone list
