# Dental Office — Patient Journey

This maps what a patient actually experiences, step by step, from deciding they need a dentist to becoming a reliable, long-term recall patient. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. They decide they need a dentist
Sometimes routine (it's been 6 months), sometimes urgent (pain, a broken tooth). Anxiety is common for both, and the tone of every interaction from here on matters more than in most other service businesses.

## 2. They look for a practice
They're checking: whether the practice takes their insurance, reviews (especially anything mentioning how gentle or communicative the staff are), and how easy it looks to book. A practice with no visible "accepts your insurance" info or unclear online presence loses anxious patients here — they'll pick the option that feels safest to try.

## 3. Booking and new-patient intake
What they need: to know what to bring (insurance card, ID), and ideally to fill out new-patient forms in advance rather than rushing through a clipboard in the waiting room. A slow, paper-heavy intake process sets a stressful tone before they've even been seen.

## 4. Insurance verification
Ideally invisible to the patient — verified before the appointment so there are no surprises about coverage on the day of. When this is skipped, patients get billing surprises after the fact, which is one of the fastest ways to lose trust in a healthcare relationship.

## 5. The appointment
The core moment. Patients are evaluating: did the provider explain what's happening and why, did they feel listened to about pain or anxiety, was the wait time reasonable, does the office feel clean and professional.

## 6. Checkout and billing
What they need: a clear explanation of what insurance covered, what they owe, and why. Confusing statements that arrive weeks later, disconnected from the visit, create frustration and can delay payment.

## 7. Recall (getting them back in 6 months)
This is the single most important repeat-business moment in a dental practice — and the easiest one to lose without noticing. A patient who isn't reminded simply drifts off the schedule, sometimes for years, without any dramatic reason.

## 8. Becoming a long-term patient / referring family
A patient who's had consistent, low-anxiety, well-communicated visits often brings their whole family to the same practice — dental care tends to be a household decision.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 7 (recall)**. It's the stage most practices lose track of quietly, and a reliable recall reminder system (see automation-opportunities in the main README) is one of the highest-leverage, lowest-cost improvements available — it keeps existing patients coming back without any new marketing spend.
