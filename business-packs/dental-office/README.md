# Dental Office Business Pack

This pack is for dental practices — general dentistry, orthodontics, or specialty practices — whether solo, small group, or a growing multi-provider office.

## Business summary

A dental office schedules appointments (often recurring cleanings plus as-needed procedures), manages patient records that are legally protected health information, coordinates with dental insurance, and depends heavily on patients actually showing up for scheduled recall visits.

## Typical goals

- fill the schedule and reduce no-shows/last-minute cancellations
- keep patients on a consistent recall schedule (cleanings every 6 months, etc.)
- handle insurance verification and claims smoothly
- protect patient health information properly (this is legally required, not optional)
- reduce front-desk administrative burden
- build trust so patients aren't anxious or avoidant about coming in

## Common pain points

- no-shows and late cancellations, especially for routine cleanings
- insurance verification and claims taking too much staff time
- patients falling off the recall schedule and not coming back for months or years
- protected health information (PHI) scattered across systems without a clear security process
- new patient intake paperwork being slow or duplicated
- billing confusion between what insurance covers and what the patient owes

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- Protected Health Information (PHI) under HIPAA — this is a hard legal requirement, not a suggestion
- dental license verification for every provider
- insurance claims and billing accuracy
- infection control and sterilization standards
- patient consent for treatment and data sharing

Before scaling up or adding digital systems, review:
- HIPAA compliance for any patient data storage, communication, or software
- who has access to patient records and why
- infection control protocols and any required certifications
- insurance verification and claims accuracy processes

## Example plan

1. confirm HIPAA compliance basics are actually in place (not just a poster in the waiting room)
2. set up a reliable recall system so patients don't silently fall off the 6-month schedule
3. build an appointment reminder system to reduce no-shows
4. streamline new-patient intake so it isn't all done with a clipboard at check-in
5. review insurance verification workflow to catch coverage issues before the appointment, not after
6. set up clear, simple patient billing statements
7. review infection control and sterilization documentation
8. launch or improve the patient-facing website and online scheduling

## Example automation opportunities

- automatic appointment reminders (text/email) to reduce no-shows
- recall reminders when a patient is due for their next cleaning/checkup
- online new-patient intake forms completed before arrival
- insurance eligibility verification run automatically before appointments
- automated billing statements and payment reminders
- review requests sent after a positive visit

## Example design direction

- calming, trustworthy visual tone (dental anxiety is real and common — the website/office should reduce it, not add to it)
- clear information on what's covered by common insurance plans, if possible
- an easy "Book an Appointment" or "New Patient" call to action
- photos of the actual office and team, which builds comfort before a first visit

## Example AI use cases

- draft patient reminder and recall message templates
- write plain-language explanations of common procedures for the website
- draft a HIPAA-compliant patient communication policy outline to review with a compliance professional
- summarize insurance plan basics into a patient-facing FAQ
- help write a simple new-patient welcome packet
