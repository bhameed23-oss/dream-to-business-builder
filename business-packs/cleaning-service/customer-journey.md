# Cleaning Service — Customer Journey

This maps what a client actually experiences, step by step, from deciding they need a cleaner to becoming a long-term recurring client. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. They decide they need help
Often triggered by a life event (new baby, moved, hosting an event) or just being overwhelmed. There's usually some hesitation about letting a stranger into their home — trust signals matter from the very first interaction.

## 2. They look for a service
They're looking for: reviews, whether the company is "bonded and insured" (a real trust phrase people search for), and how easy it is to get a price. A company with no visible pricing or trust signals loses people here.

## 3. Getting a quote
What they need: a realistic price without a hard sell, and clarity on what's included (do they need to provide supplies? does it include inside the fridge/oven?). Vague or wildly inconsistent quotes erode trust immediately.

## 4. First cleaning
This is the moment that decides if they become a repeat client. They're evaluating: did the cleaner show up on time, did the quality match what was promised, did they feel comfortable with a stranger in their space, was anything damaged or moved without care.

## 5. Access and logistics
For recurring clients, this becomes routine (a code, a key, a lockbox) — but the first time this is set up is a trust-heavy moment. Being professional and clear about how access info is handled and stored matters a lot here.

## 6. Ongoing recurring visits
The client wants: consistency (the same quality every time, ideally the same cleaner), being asked about anything specific each visit, and an easy way to reschedule without a hassle when their plans change.

## 7. Payment
Automatic recurring billing removes friction for both sides. Manual invoicing for one-off jobs needs to be fast and easy — a client who has to chase down how to pay is a client who gets annoyed.

## 8. Becoming a long-term client / referring others
A client who's had months of consistent, trustworthy service becomes extremely valuable — cleaning is a business built on retention far more than one-time jobs. Satisfied recurring clients are also a strong source of referrals, especially in a neighborhood.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 4 (first cleaning)**. It's the single moment that converts a one-time booking into a recurring client, and it's the moment where trust is won or lost. Getting the first visit right (on time, thorough, careful with the client's belongings) matters more than almost anything else in this business.
