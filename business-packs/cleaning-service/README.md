# Cleaning Service Business Pack

This pack is for residential and commercial cleaning businesses — house cleaning, office cleaning, move-in/move-out cleaning, and similar recurring-service businesses.

## Business summary

A cleaning service schedules recurring or one-time visits, sends a crew (or a solo cleaner) to a property, and needs to manage routes, supplies, quality consistency, and trust — since cleaners are working inside someone's home or business, often without the owner present.

## Typical goals

- fill the schedule with recurring (weekly/biweekly/monthly) clients, which is more stable than one-off jobs
- build routes that don't waste driving time between jobs
- keep quality consistent across different cleaners/crews
- build enough trust that clients hand over a key or door code
- get paid reliably without chasing invoices
- manage supplies and equipment across multiple job sites

## Common pain points

- last-minute cancellations or reschedules disrupting the whole day's route
- inconsistent quality between different cleaners, leading to complaints
- trust and access issues (keys, codes, alarm systems) when the client isn't home
- payment collection, especially for one-off or move-out jobs
- staff no-shows leaving a client's job uncovered
- quoting jobs accurately before ever seeing the space

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- access to a client's home or business (keys, codes, security systems)
- employee or contractor background checks (a real trust factor for this industry)
- liability and bonding insurance
- worker classification (employee vs. independent contractor) if using subcontracted cleaners
- payment information

Before scaling up, review:
- background check practices for anyone entering a client's property
- liability insurance and bonding
- worker classification if using subcontractors
- how access credentials (keys, codes) are stored and tracked

## Example plan

1. define a clear quoting process (in-person walkthrough, video call, or a detailed intake form)
2. set up recurring scheduling so regulars don't have to rebook every time
3. build a route/dispatch system so crews aren't wasting time driving
4. put a background-check process in place before anyone enters a client's home
5. get liability insurance and bonding in place
6. set up a reliable payment system (recurring billing for regulars, invoicing for one-offs)
7. create a simple quality-check process (a follow-up text or checklist)
8. launch with a clear, trustworthy-feeling booking page

## Example automation opportunities

- online quote requests with automatic follow-up
- recurring appointment scheduling and reminder texts
- automatic recurring billing for regular clients
- route optimization for crews with multiple stops a day
- post-cleaning follow-up/review requests
- staff scheduling and no-show alerts

## Example design direction

- trust-forward: photos of real (uniformed, identifiable) staff, not just stock photos of clean rooms
- clear pricing or an easy quote-request flow, since "how much does it cost" is the #1 question
- visible mentions of background checks/bonding/insurance, since that's a real differentiator for this industry
- simple recurring-booking options front and center

## Example AI use cases

- draft a quote-request follow-up sequence
- write a plain-language explanation of what "bonded and insured" means for clients
- draft a background-check policy to review with a lawyer
- summarize recurring-client notes (access instructions, preferences, pets) into one reference sheet
- write review-request messages sent after each visit
