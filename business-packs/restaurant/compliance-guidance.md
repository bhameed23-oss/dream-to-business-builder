# Restaurant — Compliance Guidance

**This is a starting point for understanding what to review, not legal advice.** Food service is one of the more heavily regulated small business categories — talk to a lawyer and your local health department for anything specific to your city, county, or state.

## Data you're likely handling

- Customer info (if you run a loyalty program or online ordering accounts)
- Payment information
- Employee personal and payroll information

## Areas to review

1. **Food safety codes** — Most states and localities base their food safety rules on the FDA Food Code, a model set of standards for food handling, storage, and preparation. Your local health department enforces the specific version adopted in your area — check with them directly, or search "[your city/county] health department food safety" for their exact requirements.
2. **Food handler certification** — Many states require staff who handle food to hold a certification. ServSafe (servsafe.com) is the most widely recognized program in the U.S., though some states have their own equivalent — check with your local health department.
3. **Liquor licensing** — If you serve alcohol, licensing is handled at the state level, usually through a state Alcohol Beverage Control (ABC) agency or liquor control board. Requirements and server training rules vary significantly by state — search "[your state] ABC" or "[your state] liquor control board."
4. **Labor law (breaks, overtime, tips)** — The federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) sets baseline rules for overtime pay and how "tip credit" wages can work (paying tipped employees a lower base wage, with tips making up the difference, under specific conditions). The U.S. Department of Labor publishes plain-language guidance at dol.gov. Many states have their own, sometimes stricter, rules on top of the federal floor — check your state labor department too.
5. **Minor labor laws** — If you employ anyone under 18, both federal and state law restrict how many hours and which hours they can work, especially on school nights. The Department of Labor's Youth Rules site (dol.gov) covers the federal rules; check your state labor department for anything stricter.
6. **Health inspections** — These typically happen on a recurring schedule (varies by jurisdiction and risk category) plus in response to complaints. Ask your local health department how often to expect an inspection and what triggers a re-inspection.

## When to bring in a professional

- Before applying for or renewing a liquor license
- If a health inspection results in a serious violation or closure order
- If you're unsure whether your tip-pooling or tip-credit setup follows federal and state wage law
