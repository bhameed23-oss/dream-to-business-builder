# Restaurant — Tools and Integrations

Real tools, roughly ordered from "free and simple" to "paid and full-featured." Start at the top — don't buy an enterprise restaurant management suite before you've outgrown a basic POS.

## Point of sale (the core system)

- **Square for Restaurants** — Free to start (with paid tiers for more features), widely used by smaller restaurants and cafes. Handles orders, payments, and basic reporting.
- **Toast** — Paid, purpose-built for restaurants specifically, popular with full-service and quick-service restaurants that need deeper features (kitchen display systems, detailed labor reporting).
- **Clover** — Paid, another common restaurant POS option, similar territory to Square.

## Online ordering and delivery

- **Square Online** or **Toast's** built-in online ordering — Direct ordering that avoids third-party app commissions, worth setting up even if you also use delivery apps.
- **DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub** — Real, widely used third-party delivery platforms. They bring order volume but take a real commission (often 15-30%) — worth comparing against building direct online ordering for regulars.

## Inventory and food cost

- **MarketMan** or **Toast's inventory features** — Paid, purpose-built for tracking food cost and reducing waste; worth adopting once manual inventory counts become unreliable.
- **A shared spreadsheet** — Free, fine for a small operation just starting to track waste and reorder points.

## Staff scheduling

- **7shifts** — Paid, built specifically for restaurant scheduling, including shift swaps and labor-cost forecasting.
- **When I Work** — Paid alternative, also popular for hourly/shift-based scheduling across industries including restaurants.

## Reviews and reputation

- **Google Business Profile** (free) — Set this up first, with an accurate, current menu and photos. It's one of the first things a potential customer checks.

## Food safety

- **ServSafe** (servsafe.com) — The most widely recognized food handler and manager certification program in the U.S.; many states/counties require or strongly recommend it.

## Security and record-keeping basics

Because payment info and staff scheduling/pay data are involved:
- Use a real payment processor rather than manually keying and storing card numbers
- Limit who has access to sales and payroll data
- See the compliance-guidance.md file in this folder for food safety and labor law basics
