# Restaurant Business Pack

This pack is for restaurants, cafes, food trucks, and similar food-service businesses — dine-in, takeout, delivery, or a mix.

## Business summary

A restaurant takes orders (in person, by phone, or online), prepares and serves food, manages inventory that spoils if mismanaged, staffs a kitchen and front-of-house, and handles some of the strictest regulatory requirements of any small business (food safety, health inspections, liquor licensing if applicable).

## Typical goals

- keep tables/seats full (or orders flowing, for takeout/delivery-focused places)
- manage food cost and reduce waste
- keep staffing reliable across kitchen and front-of-house
- build repeat customers instead of relying only on new foot traffic
- handle third-party delivery apps without losing too much margin
- pass health inspections consistently, not just scramble before one

## Common pain points

- food waste and inventory going bad before it's used
- inconsistent staffing (no-shows, high turnover, scheduling conflicts)
- delivery app fees eating into margin
- inconsistent food quality when a different cook is on shift
- slow or inaccurate order-taking during rush periods
- cash flow being tight even when the dining room looks busy

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- food safety and handling (a top health-inspection concern)
- alcohol service, if applicable (a separate license and serving rules)
- employee scheduling and labor law (overtime, breaks, minor work-hour rules if hiring under 18)
- tip pooling and reporting
- payment processing across in-person, phone, and app orders

Before scaling up, review:
- food handler certification requirements for staff
- health department inspection history and standards
- liquor license status and server training requirements, if serving alcohol
- labor law compliance for scheduling and tips

## Example plan

1. tighten the food safety and handling process (staff certification, temperature logs)
2. set up a reliable POS (point of sale) system that handles dine-in, takeout, and delivery in one place
3. build an inventory process to reduce waste and catch shortages before they cause a menu problem
4. create a staffing/scheduling system that reduces no-shows and last-minute gaps
5. decide which third-party delivery apps are actually worth the fee, versus building direct online ordering
6. set up a simple loyalty or repeat-customer mechanism
7. review labor law compliance (breaks, overtime, tip handling)
8. launch or relaunch with a strong Google Business Profile and real menu photos

## Example automation opportunities

- online ordering and reservation confirmations
- inventory/reorder alerts when stock runs low
- staff scheduling reminders and shift-swap requests
- loyalty program point tracking and rewards
- review requests after a positive experience
- automatic reconciliation of orders across in-house POS and delivery apps

## Example design direction

- mouth-watering, real photography of actual dishes (not generic stock food photos)
- an easy-to-find menu, hours, and "Order Now" or "Reserve a Table" action
- clear indication of dine-in vs. takeout vs. delivery options
- a personality that matches the restaurant's actual vibe (fast-casual vs. fine dining look very different)

## Example AI use cases

- draft a food-safety checklist for opening/closing shifts
- write staff scheduling reminder templates
- summarize customer reviews to spot recurring complaints or praise
- draft social captions for daily specials
- help write a simple standard operating procedure for a new hire
