# Restaurant — Customer Journey

This maps what a customer actually experiences, step by step, from deciding where to eat to becoming a regular. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. They decide to eat out (or order in)
Often driven by hunger, convenience, or a specific craving. Speed of decision matters — if it takes too long to find your menu or hours online, they move to the next option.

## 2. They look you up
They're checking: the menu (with prices), recent reviews, hours, and photos of the actual food. A restaurant with an outdated menu online or no recent photos loses people here, even if the food itself is great.

## 3. Ordering or arriving
For dine-in: how quickly they're seated and how the first interaction feels. For takeout/delivery: how easy the ordering process is, and whether the estimated time is accurate. A confusing menu or a broken online-ordering flow costs real orders.

## 4. The meal
This is the core moment, but it's not just about taste — speed, temperature, order accuracy, and whether staff seem attentive all shape whether this becomes a repeat visit. A great dish delivered cold or an order that's wrong undoes a lot of good work elsewhere.

## 5. Payment and departure
The customer wants this to be fast and clear, especially at the end of a meal. Confusing bills, slow checkout, or an awkward tipping prompt sour an otherwise good experience right at the end.

## 6. After the visit
A receipt or app that makes it easy to leave a review, or a loyalty program that quietly reminds them to come back, extends the relationship past a single visit.

## 7. Becoming a regular
Regulars are the backbone of most restaurants' actual profitability — they cost nothing to acquire and often order more confidently (bigger tabs, less menu hesitation). Consistency across visits (same quality, same friendliness) is what turns a good first meal into a habit.

## 8. Referring others
Happy regulars bring friends, especially for special occasions. This is where a restaurant's local reputation compounds — but only if the consistency from stage 7 holds up.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 4 (the meal)** — specifically order accuracy and consistency. It sounds obvious, but inconsistent execution (a dish that's great one visit and off the next) is one of the most common reasons a customer who liked you once doesn't come back. A tight kitchen process and a food-safety/quality checklist (see review-checklist.md) protect this stage directly.
