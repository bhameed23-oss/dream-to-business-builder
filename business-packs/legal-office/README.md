# Legal Office Business Pack

This pack is for small to mid-size law firms and solo practitioners — general practice, family law, immigration, personal injury, estate planning, or similar client-facing legal services.

## Business summary

A law office intakes new client matters, manages deadlines and case files (often with serious consequences for missed deadlines), bills for time or flat fees, and operates under strict professional and ethical rules set by the state bar — not just ordinary business regulations.

## Typical goals

- convert consultations into signed clients without a slow, confusing intake process
- track case deadlines reliably (missed deadlines can be malpractice, not just an inconvenience)
- bill accurately and get paid without excessive follow-up
- keep client communication organized and responsive
- protect attorney-client privileged information properly
- manage a caseload without any matter falling through the cracks

## Common pain points

- new client intake being slow, inconsistent, or losing potential clients before they ever sign
- deadline tracking relying on memory or a single person's calendar
- time tracking and billing being tedious, leading to under-billed hours
- client communication scattered across email, phone, and text with no central record
- conflict-of-interest checks being informal instead of systematic
- trust account (IOLTA) recordkeeping being a source of stress rather than routine

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- attorney-client privileged and confidential information
- state bar ethics rules (advertising, fee agreements, conflicts of interest)
- trust accounting (IOLTA) rules, which carry serious professional consequences if mishandled
- court and statutory deadlines
- client payment and fee agreements

Before scaling up or adding new systems, review:
- state bar rules on advertising, referrals, and fee agreements
- trust account (IOLTA) compliance
- conflict-of-interest checking process
- confidentiality protections for any software touching client data

## Example plan

1. build a consistent intake process so no potential client is lost to a slow response
2. set up a reliable conflict-of-interest checking system before any matter is accepted
3. implement (or tighten) a deadline-tracking/calendaring system with redundancy, not a single point of failure
4. review trust accounting (IOLTA) practices for compliance
5. streamline time tracking so billable hours aren't lost to poor habits
6. create clear, simple fee agreements and client communication templates
7. set up a centralized client communication record
8. launch or improve intake forms and a clear, professional web presence

## Example automation opportunities

- online intake forms with automatic conflict-check flagging
- deadline and calendaring reminders with built-in redundancy
- time-tracking prompts so billable work isn't missed
- automated invoice generation and payment reminders
- client status update templates for common case stages
- document assembly for routine, repeatable filings

## Example design direction

- professional, trustworthy tone — this is one of the highest-stakes decisions a client makes, and the site should reflect seriousness and competence
- clear practice area descriptions in plain language, not just legal jargon
- an easy, low-friction way to request a consultation
- attorney bios that build credibility (background, results where ethically permitted to share, reviews)

## Example AI use cases

- draft client intake questionnaires for a specific practice area
- write plain-language explanations of legal processes for the website (not legal advice — general process explanations)
- draft client status update templates for common case milestones
- summarize case notes into a clean internal case status reference
- draft a conflict-of-interest intake checklist to review with the firm's ethics counsel
