# Legal Office — Tools and Integrations

Real tools, roughly ordered from simpler/lower-cost to full-featured practice management platforms. Because this industry handles privileged, confidential client information and regulated trust funds, tool choice here has real ethical and compliance implications — see compliance-guidance.md before adopting anything new.

## Practice management (the core system)

- **Clio** — Paid, one of the most widely used legal practice management platforms, handling case management, time tracking, billing, and often trust accounting in one system.
- **MyCase** — Paid alternative to Clio, similar core feature set, popular with small to mid-size firms.
- **PracticePanther** — Paid alternative, also widely used, with strong billing and client portal features.

All three of the above are built with legal-specific features (conflict checking, trust accounting, deadline calendaring) as a core part of the product — worth prioritizing over a generic project-management tool for this reason.

## Deadline and calendaring redundancy

- Most of the practice management systems above include deadline/calendar features with reminders. Because a missed deadline can be a malpractice issue, many firms deliberately build in redundancy — a shared firm calendar in addition to individual calendars, so no single person's absence creates a gap.

## Trust accounting (IOLTA)

- Clio, MyCase, and PracticePanther all offer trust accounting features built to help track client trust funds separately from operating funds, which state bar rules require. This is one of the areas where using purpose-built software (rather than a general accounting tool) is particularly valuable, given the professional consequences of trust accounting errors.

## Document assembly and e-signature

- **DocuSign** — Widely used for client signatures on engagement letters and other documents.
- Document assembly features built into Clio, MyCase, and similar platforms can speed up routine, repeatable filings.

## Client communication and intake

- Online intake forms (built into most practice management platforms, or standalone tools) speed up the first-contact-to-signed-client process, which is one of the most common places potential clients are lost.
- A client portal (also commonly built into these platforms) centralizes communication and document sharing, reducing the "everything is scattered across email" problem.

## Reviews and reputation

- **Google Business Profile** and legal-specific review platforms like **Avvo** — Reviews carry significant weight in a decision as high-stakes as choosing an attorney.

## Security basics for this industry

Because attorney-client privileged and confidential information is involved:
- Any software storing client communications or documents should have strong access controls and encryption
- Access to client files should be limited to attorneys and staff actually working that matter
- See the compliance-guidance.md file in this folder before adopting any new client-facing communication or storage tool
