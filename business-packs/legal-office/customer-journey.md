# Legal Office — Client Journey

This maps what a client actually experiences, step by step, from realizing they need a lawyer to becoming a satisfied, referring former client. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. They realize they need legal help
Often stressful (a divorce, an arrest, an injury, a business dispute) or high-stakes (estate planning, a business deal). Clients are frequently anxious, unfamiliar with the process, and unsure what things will cost — clarity and empathy matter enormously here.

## 2. They look for an attorney
They're checking: reviews, whether the practice area matches their exact need, and whether contacting the firm feels approachable rather than intimidating. A firm with a confusing website or no clear "how much does this cost" information loses anxious clients here.

## 3. First contact / consultation
What they need: to feel heard, a realistic sense of their options, and clarity on cost (flat fee, hourly, contingency, retainer). A slow response to a first inquiry is one of the most common reasons a potential client goes elsewhere — legal problems often feel urgent even when they aren't technically time-sensitive.

## 4. Signing on (engagement)
The client wants: a clear fee agreement they actually understand, and to know what happens next and roughly when. Confusing engagement letters or an unclear retainer process create doubt right at the start of the relationship.

## 5. The matter in progress
This is where trust is built or lost. Clients want: regular updates (even "nothing new to report yet" is better than silence), plain-language explanations of what's happening, and confidence that deadlines are being tracked. Radio silence during a long case is one of the top sources of legal-client dissatisfaction, even when the underlying work is being done well.

## 6. Billing
The client wants: bills that clearly explain what they're paying for, without unpleasant surprises. Detailed, well-organized invoices reduce disputes and speed up payment.

## 7. Resolution
Whether it's a settlement, a closing, a filed document, or a verdict, the client wants a clear explanation of the outcome and what (if anything) happens next.

## 8. After the matter closes
A closing letter, a check-in, or simply being available for a quick follow-up question leaves clients with a good final impression — and is often the moment they decide whether to refer you to someone else.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 5 (the matter in progress)** — specifically proactive client communication. Building in regular status updates (even automated ones for common milestones) is one of the highest-leverage improvements available, since most client dissatisfaction in legal services comes from feeling forgotten, not from the legal work itself.
