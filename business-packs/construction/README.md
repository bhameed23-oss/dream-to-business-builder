# Construction Business Pack

This pack is for general contractors, home remodelers, and specialty trade businesses (electrical, plumbing, roofing, HVAC, etc.) — whether solo, a small crew, or managing subcontractors across multiple job sites.

## Business summary

A construction business estimates jobs, schedules crews and subcontractors across multiple sites, manages materials and permits, and depends on accurate bidding since a bad estimate can turn a profitable job into a loss. This is also one of the more physically risky industries, which shapes a lot of what needs to be reviewed.

## Typical goals

- estimate jobs accurately so pricing covers real cost plus margin
- keep crews and subcontractors scheduled efficiently across job sites
- manage materials and avoid costly delays waiting on supplies
- get permits and inspections handled without derailing the timeline
- get paid on schedule, including progress payments on larger jobs
- keep the jobsite safe and reduce liability exposure

## Common pain points

- estimates that don't hold up once the real cost of materials/labor comes in
- payment delays, especially waiting on progress payments or final payment after completion
- permit and inspection delays throwing off the whole project schedule
- subcontractor scheduling conflicts and no-shows
- material costs changing between bid and purchase
- disputes over change orders (work added or changed after the original bid)

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- contractor licensing (general and trade-specific)
- worker classification (employee vs. subcontractor)
- jobsite safety (OSHA requirements)
- permits and building code compliance
- liability and workers' compensation insurance
- lien rights and payment protection

Before scaling up, review:
- licensing requirements for the type of work performed
- worker classification for any subcontractors used
- jobsite safety practices and required training
- insurance coverage (general liability, workers' comp)
- contract language covering change orders and payment terms

## Example plan

1. tighten the estimating process so bids reliably reflect real costs
2. set up a clear, written contract template covering scope, payment schedule, and change orders
3. build a scheduling system that coordinates crews/subcontractors across job sites
4. review permit and inspection processes for the jurisdictions you work in
5. confirm insurance coverage (general liability, workers' comp) is current and adequate
6. review worker classification for any subcontractors
7. set up a jobsite safety checklist and required training tracking
8. launch or improve a portfolio-driven website showing real completed work

## Example automation opportunities

- automated progress payment invoicing tied to project milestones
- material ordering reminders tied to project schedule
- subcontractor scheduling and confirmation reminders
- change order documentation and client sign-off tracking
- permit/inspection deadline tracking
- review requests after project completion

## Example design direction

- a strong photo portfolio of real completed projects (before/after is especially persuasive)
- clear service area and specialties (kitchen remodels, roofing, electrical, etc.)
- visible licensing/insurance information, since that's a real trust and legal differentiator
- an easy way to request an estimate

## Example AI use cases

- draft a clear, plain-language contract outline covering scope and payment terms (to review with a lawyer)
- write progress-update templates for clients during longer projects
- draft a jobsite safety checklist
- summarize project notes into a client-facing status update
- help write change-order documentation language
