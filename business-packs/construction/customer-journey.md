# Construction — Client Journey

This maps what a client actually experiences, step by step, from deciding to start a project to becoming a referral source. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. They decide to start a project
Often a significant financial decision (a remodel, a new roof, an addition) and sometimes urgent (storm damage, a failed inspection). Clients are usually nervous about cost overruns and contractor reliability — reputation matters enormously before they ever make contact.

## 2. They look for a contractor
They're checking: a portfolio of real completed work (especially before/after photos), reviews, and whether licensing/insurance is clearly stated. A contractor with no visible portfolio or unclear credentials loses cautious clients here.

## 3. Getting an estimate
What they need: a clear, itemized bid they can actually understand, and confidence that the final cost won't wildly diverge from it. Vague, verbal-only estimates are one of the fastest ways to lose a client's trust before work even starts.

## 4. Signing a contract
The client wants: clear scope of work, a clear payment schedule, and clear language on how changes (change orders) will be handled and priced. This is also where lien rights and payment terms should be spelled out, protecting both sides.

## 5. During the project
This is where trust is built or lost over time. Clients want: regular updates (especially when something changes or is delayed), a clean and safe jobsite, and confidence that permits/inspections are being handled properly. Silence during a multi-week project is one of the top sources of client anxiety, even when the work itself is on track.

## 6. Change orders
Handled well, these feel like a normal part of a flexible process. Handled poorly (verbal agreements, unclear pricing), they become the single biggest source of disputes in construction. Written, signed change orders protect both the client and the business.

## 7. Completion and final payment
The client wants: a clear final walkthrough, confirmation that everything matches what was agreed, and a straightforward final invoice. Payment delays often happen here when the client isn't fully satisfied or confused about what's owed.

## 8. After the project / referring others
A well-executed project with good communication throughout is one of the strongest referral generators in local business — construction work is highly visible (a new kitchen, a new roof) and word of mouth travels fast in a neighborhood.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 5 (during the project)** — specifically proactive communication. Regular, even brief, updates during a project dramatically reduce client anxiety and disputes, and are one of the cheapest improvements available relative to their impact on referrals and repeat business.
