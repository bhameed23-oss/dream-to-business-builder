# Auto Body Shop — Compliance Guidance

**This is a starting point for understanding what to review, not legal advice.** Talk to a lawyer or insurance professional for anything specific to your state or situation.

## Data you're likely handling

- Customer name, phone, email, address
- Vehicle VIN, license plate, registration info
- Insurance policy and claim numbers
- Payment information

## Areas to review before you scale up digital record-keeping

1. **Storage security** — If you're keeping records digitally, are they password-protected? Who can access them?
2. **Insurance coordination** — If you communicate with insurance companies on a customer's behalf, do you have their written authorization to do so?
3. **Payment handling** — Are you using a real payment processor (card reader, Square, etc.) rather than manually keying in and storing card numbers yourself?
4. **Document retention** — How long do you keep estimates, photos, and repair records? Some states have minimum requirements for auto repair shops specifically.
5. **State-specific auto repair regulations** — Many states have specific disclosure requirements for repair shops (written estimates, parts disclosure — OEM vs. aftermarket — customer authorization before starting work). Search "[your state] auto repair shop regulations" or check with your state's Department of Motor Vehicles or Attorney General's consumer protection office.

## When to bring in a professional

- Before you start doing insurance-direct billing at any real volume
- If you're not sure whether your state requires a written estimate before starting repairs
- If a customer disputes a bill and it's heading toward a formal complaint or legal issue
