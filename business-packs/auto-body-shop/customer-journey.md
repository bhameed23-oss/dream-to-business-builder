# Auto Body Shop — Customer Journey

This maps what a customer actually experiences, step by step, from the moment something goes wrong with their car to the moment they'd recommend you to a friend. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. Something happens (accident, damage, cosmetic issue)
The customer is often stressed, sometimes dealing with insurance for the first time. Tone matters more here than almost anywhere else in the journey — this is not a routine purchase for them.

## 2. They search for a shop
They're looking for: trust signals (reviews, before/after photos), clear pricing expectations, and how fast they can get an estimate. A slow or unclear website loses customers here.

## 3. First contact
Phone call, form, or walk-in. What they need: to feel heard, a realistic timeline for an estimate, and to know what to bring (insurance info, registration).

## 4. Estimate and intake
The customer wants: an honest number, a clear explanation of what's being fixed and why, and to know when they'll get their car back. This is the moment that builds or breaks trust.

## 5. Waiting during the repair
This is where most shops lose customers to frustration — not because the repair is bad, but because the customer doesn't know what's happening. Proactive status updates (even simple ones) matter enormously here.

## 6. Pickup and payment
The customer wants: no surprises on the bill, a clean car, and a clear explanation if anything changed from the original estimate.

## 7. After the repair
Follow-up (a call, text, or email) asking if everything is holding up well. This is also the natural moment to ask for a review — but only after confirming they're satisfied.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 5 (waiting during repair)**. It's the stage most shops handle worst, and proactive communication here is one of the cheapest, highest-impact automations available (see automation-opportunities in the main README).
