# Nonprofit — Compliance Guidance

**This is a starting point for understanding what to review, not legal advice.** Nonprofit compliance spans federal tax law, state charity regulation, and governance law — talk to a nonprofit attorney or accountant for anything specific to your organization.

## Data you're likely handling

- Donor names, contact information, and giving history
- Beneficiary/program participant information
- Volunteer personal information (and background check results, if applicable)
- Payment information

## Areas to review

1. **501(c)(3) status maintenance** — Tax-exempt status isn't a one-time achievement; it requires ongoing compliance, including filing an annual return. The IRS publishes guidance for exempt organizations at irs.gov/charities-non-profits, including what activities could jeopardize exempt status (excessive lobbying, private benefit to insiders, unrelated business activity beyond certain limits).
2. **Form 990 filing** — Most tax-exempt organizations must file an annual information return (Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N depending on size) with the IRS. Missing this filing for three consecutive years results in automatic revocation of tax-exempt status — a serious, sometimes hard-to-reverse consequence. Check irs.gov for the specific form and deadline that applies to your organization's size.
3. **State charitable solicitation registration** — Most states require nonprofits to register before soliciting donations from residents of that state, and many require it before fundraising online reaches residents there too. Requirements and renewal schedules vary significantly by state — search "[your state] charitable solicitation registration" or check the National Association of State Charity Officials (nasconet.org) for links to each state's requirements.
4. **Unrelated Business Income Tax (UBIT)** — If a nonprofit earns income from an activity not substantially related to its exempt purpose (e.g., running an unrelated retail operation), that income may be subject to UBIT. The IRS publishes guidance on what counts (irs.gov, search "unrelated business income tax").
5. **Board governance and fiduciary duty** — Board members have legal fiduciary duties (care, loyalty, obedience to the mission) under state nonprofit corporation law. A documented conflict-of-interest policy, regular meetings with minutes, and real financial oversight are standard expectations, not optional formalities.
6. **Volunteer background checks** — If volunteers work with children, elderly people, or other vulnerable populations, background checks are considered standard practice and are sometimes legally required depending on the state and the specific program (e.g., programs receiving certain federal or state funding).

## When to bring in a professional

- Before starting any activity that could be considered outside the organization's stated exempt purpose
- If the organization has missed a Form 990 filing in a prior year
- Before fundraising in a new state, to confirm charitable solicitation registration requirements there
