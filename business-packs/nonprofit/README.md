# Nonprofit Business Pack

This pack is for nonprofit organizations — charities, community organizations, foundations, and mission-driven groups, typically operating as 501(c)(3) or similar tax-exempt entities. It's written in the same practical style as the other packs, adapted for an organization whose "customer" is really a mix of donors, beneficiaries, volunteers, and a board.

## Business summary

A nonprofit raises funds (donations, grants, events), delivers a mission-driven program or service, manages volunteers and often a small paid staff, and answers to a board of directors and, ultimately, public trust — since nonprofit finances and impact are expected to be more transparent than a typical private business.

## Typical goals

- build a reliable, diversified funding base (not dependent on one grant or one big donor)
- keep donor relationships warm so people give again, not just once
- run programs effectively and be able to show real impact
- manage volunteers so the mission doesn't depend entirely on paid staff
- stay compliant with nonprofit-specific tax and reporting rules
- keep the board engaged and informed without every meeting being a fire drill

## Common pain points

- funding that's too dependent on a single grant or donor, creating instability
- donor management that's informal (a spreadsheet, or worse, memory) instead of a real system
- volunteer coordination that's inconsistent, leading to burnout or gaps in coverage
- unclear or hard-to-explain impact, making fundraising harder
- board members who are engaged in name only, not actually helping
- required annual filings (like Form 990) becoming a last-minute scramble

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- 501(c)(3) or other tax-exempt status maintenance
- donor and grant compliance (restricted vs. unrestricted funds)
- required annual filings (Form 990 and state-level equivalents)
- board governance responsibilities
- volunteer management and, if applicable, background checks for volunteers working with vulnerable populations

Before scaling fundraising or programs, review:
- tax-exempt status compliance and required filings
- how restricted grant funds are tracked separately from general funds
- board governance structure and meeting/reporting cadence
- volunteer screening if working with children, elderly, or other vulnerable populations

## Example plan

1. build a real donor management system so relationships aren't tracked in someone's head
2. diversify funding sources so the organization isn't dependent on one grant renewing
3. set up a clear, simple way to show programmatic impact (numbers, stories, outcomes)
4. review board governance — meeting cadence, clear roles, financial oversight
5. confirm required filings (Form 990, state charitable registration) are current and not last-minute
6. build a volunteer recruitment and coordination process
7. set up restricted-fund tracking separate from general operating funds
8. launch or improve a donor-facing website with a clear, easy way to give

## Example automation opportunities

- automated donor thank-you and receipt emails
- recurring donation reminders and renewal prompts
- volunteer shift scheduling and reminder texts
- grant deadline and reporting-requirement tracking
- impact reporting pulled together automatically from program data
- board meeting reminder and document-sharing automation

## Example design direction

- an emotionally clear mission statement and story, since donors give to people and outcomes, not organizational charts
- a simple, prominent "Donate" action
- real photos/stories from the actual program, not generic stock imagery
- transparency signals (how funds are used, impact numbers) since trust drives giving

## Example AI use cases

- draft donor thank-you and impact-update templates
- write a plain-language summary of program impact for a grant report
- draft a volunteer onboarding checklist
- summarize board meeting notes into clear action items
- help draft a case for support (the core fundraising pitch document)
