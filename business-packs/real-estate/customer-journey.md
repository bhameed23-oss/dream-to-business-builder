# Real Estate — Customer Journey

Mapped from a buyer's or seller's perspective. Use this to find where leads go cold or clients feel unsupported.

## 1. First interest
A lead sees a listing, gets a referral, or fills out a form. They're often just browsing at this stage — expecting a hard sales pitch too early can push them away.

## 2. First contact
Speed matters enormously here. A lead that isn't contacted within the first hour is dramatically less likely to convert than one contacted immediately. This is the single highest-leverage moment in the entire journey.

## 3. Qualification
Understanding what they actually want (timeline, budget, must-haves) versus what they said in the initial inquiry, which is often vague. This is a conversation, not a form.

## 4. Active search / listing prep
For buyers: touring properties, narrowing preferences. For sellers: preparing the home, setting price expectations. Clients want to feel guided, not just shown a list of options.

## 5. Offer / negotiation
High-stress, time-sensitive. Clients need clear, fast communication about what's happening and why. This is where trust is won or lost the hardest.

## 6. Under contract
Coordinating inspections, lenders, title work, and disclosures. Clients often don't understand this stage and can feel anxious if they don't know what's happening next.

## 7. Closing
The final signatures and handoff. Clients want this to feel smooth and celebratory, not stressful and confusing.

## 8. After closing
Follow-up, staying in touch, asking for referrals or reviews. This is where most agents drop off entirely — and where repeat business and referrals actually come from.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage, fix **stage 2 (first contact speed)**. It's the most measurable, most automatable, and has the clearest link to whether a lead becomes a client at all.
