# Barber Shop — Tools and Integrations

Real tools, roughly ordered from "free and simple" to "paid and full-featured." Start at the top — don't buy a full booking/management platform before you've outgrown a shared calendar.

## Booking and scheduling (the core system)

- **A shared Google Calendar** — Free. Fine for a single-barber shop just starting out, or while testing whether online booking even gets used.
- **Booksy** — Paid, extremely popular specifically with barbers and salons. Handles online booking, reminders, client records, and even lets clients discover new barbers nearby. Widely used in this industry.
- **Square Appointments** — Paid (with a free tier for a single location/user), integrates booking directly with Square's payment processing, which matters if you're already using Square for checkout.
- **Vagaro** or **StyleSeat** — Also popular, similar feature sets to Booksy; worth comparing pricing since they change over time.

## Payment and tipping

- **Square** or **Clover** — Card readers that also support built-in tip prompts at checkout, which tends to increase both tip frequency and amount compared to a cash jar.

## Client communication and reminders

- Most of the booking tools above (Booksy, Square Appointments, Vagaro) include automatic text reminders and rebooking nudges — this is usually the single highest-value feature for a barber shop, worth prioritizing when choosing a tool.

## Reviews and reputation

- **Google Business Profile** (free) — Set this up first. It's how most new clients will find and vet a shop before ever walking in.

## For booth-rental shops specifically

- A simple recurring invoice or rent-collection feature (Square, Booksy, and Vagaro all offer some version of this) makes chasing down rent far less awkward than doing it in cash or by text.
- A written booth-rental agreement template — search for one through your state bar association's small-business resources, or have a local small-business attorney draft one; this is not something to improvise, since it affects whether a barber is legally an employee or an independent contractor.

## Security and record-keeping basics

Because client contact info and payment details are involved:
- Use a real payment processor rather than manually keying and storing card numbers yourself
- Use a password manager for any shared shop logins
- See the compliance-guidance.md file in this folder before deciding how long to keep client records
