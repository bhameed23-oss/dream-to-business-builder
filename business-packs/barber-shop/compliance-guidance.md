# Barber Shop — Compliance Guidance

**This is a starting point for understanding what to review, not legal advice.** Talk to a lawyer or your state's barber licensing board for anything specific to your state or situation — licensing and health rules for barbering are set at the state (and sometimes city/county) level and vary a lot.

## Data you're likely handling

- Client name, phone, email
- Client preferences/history (what they usually get, allergies to certain products)
- Payment information

## Areas to review

1. **Barber licensing** — Nearly every state requires an individual license to cut hair professionally, and the shop itself often needs a separate establishment license. Requirements (hours of training, exams, renewal) vary by state. Start with your state's cosmetology/barber licensing board — most states have one, and it's usually reachable through a ".gov" search for "[your state] board of barbering" or "[your state] board of cosmetology."
2. **Health and sanitation rules** — Local or state health departments typically set rules for sanitizing tools, chairs, and stations between clients. Ask your local health department directly, or check their website, before opening.
3. **Bloodborne pathogen exposure** — If any barber in the shop uses straight razors or does any service where a nick could draw blood, the federal Occupational Safety and Health Administration's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030) is worth reading — it covers safe handling and disposal of anything that could carry blood. See osha.gov for the full standard and plain-language guidance.
4. **Worker classification (employee vs. independent contractor)** — This matters a lot for booth-rental shops. Misclassifying an employee as an independent contractor can create real tax and legal exposure. The IRS publishes guidance on how this distinction is made (search "IRS independent contractor vs employee" at irs.gov), and some barbers or shop owners request an official determination using IRS Form SS-8 if it's unclear.
5. **Booth-rental agreements** — If you rent chairs to independent barbers, a written agreement (rent amount, schedule, hours, what's included, who owns the client relationship) protects both sides. This should be reviewed by a local small-business attorney rather than copied from a generic template, since it interacts with the worker-classification question above.
6. **Liability insurance** — General liability insurance (and sometimes professional liability) covers things like a slip-and-fall or a product reaction. Ask a licensed insurance agent what's typical and required in your state.

## When to bring in a professional

- Before signing any booth-rental agreement
- If you're not sure whether the barbers in your shop should be classified as employees or independent contractors
- Before opening, to confirm every required license and inspection is actually in place (not just "in progress")
