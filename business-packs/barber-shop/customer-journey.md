# Barber Shop — Customer Journey

This maps what a client actually experiences, step by step, from deciding they need a cut to becoming a loyal regular. Use it to find where the experience breaks down.

## 1. They realize they need a cut
Sometimes planned (every 3-4 weeks, like clockwork for a regular), sometimes urgent (an event tomorrow). The booking experience needs to work for both.

## 2. They look for a barber
New clients look at: photos of actual work (fades, beard lines, the space itself), reviews, and how easy it looks to book. A shop with no visible booking option or no recent photos loses people here before they ever call.

## 3. Booking
What they need: to see real availability (not "call and hope"), a clear price range, and confirmation they actually have an appointment. If booking requires five back-and-forth texts, many people give up and go somewhere with an online calendar.

## 4. The visit
This is where trust and loyalty actually get built. A new client is quietly evaluating: did the barber ask what they wanted instead of assuming, does the cut match what was discussed, is the chat comfortable, is the shop clean. A regular client is evaluating: does this barber remember what they usually get.

## 5. Payment and tipping
The client wants this to be fast and not awkward. A tipping process that's built into checkout (rather than an uncomfortable cash fumble) tends to increase both tip amounts and how good the visit feels afterward.

## 6. Rebooking
The single highest-leverage moment in this whole journey. A client who books their next appointment before leaving the chair is a client who comes back on schedule. A client who leaves without rebooking is a client who might drift to whoever's most convenient in 5 weeks.

## 7. Between visits
A simple reminder text ("it's been about 4 weeks since your last cut with [barber]") brings back clients who intended to rebook but got busy. This is one of the cheapest, most effective things a shop can automate.

## 8. Becoming a regular / referring others
Satisfied, consistent clients refer friends and family — but usually only if asked, or if leaving a review is made easy right after a good visit.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 6 (rebooking)**. Getting the next appointment on the books before the client leaves the chair — or automating a reminder shortly after — is the single biggest lever for keeping a full schedule without constantly chasing new clients.
