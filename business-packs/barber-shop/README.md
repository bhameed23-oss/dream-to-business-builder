# Barber Shop Business Pack

This pack is for barber shops, men's grooming studios, and combination barber/salon spaces — whether it's a single chair or a multi-barber shop with booth rentals.

## Business summary

A barber shop books appointments (or takes walk-ins), manages a schedule across one or more barbers, handles payment and tipping, and keeps clients coming back through consistency and relationship. Many shops also rent chairs to independent barbers rather than employing them directly, which changes how money, scheduling, and responsibility work.

## Typical goals

- fill the chair(s) with a steady, predictable schedule
- reduce no-shows and last-minute cancellations
- make booking easy (online, by text, or by walk-in)
- keep client history and preferences (fade length, last visit, products used)
- get paid smoothly, including tips
- build a loyal client base that rebooks without being asked
- if renting chairs: track rent collection and keep each barber's business separate

## Common pain points

- no-shows and late cancellations eating into the day
- booking happening across too many channels (texts, calls, walk-ins, DMs) with nothing centralized
- new clients not knowing what a barber specializes in or what to expect
- inconsistent client records — a new barber has no idea what the regular usually gets
- cash handling and tips being hard to track
- if it's a booth-rental shop: unclear agreements about rent, hours, and who owns the client relationship

## Important review areas

This business often touches:
- a state or local barber license (for the shop and for each barber cutting hair)
- health and sanitation rules (tools, chairs, and stations)
- if renting chairs to independent barbers: worker classification (employee vs. independent contractor) and a written booth-rental agreement
- client payment information and tipping
- liability insurance (a nick or reaction to a product is rare, but it happens)

Before opening or going live with a booking system, review:
- your state's barber licensing board requirements (for the shop and each barber)
- local health department sanitation rules
- if applicable, booth-rental agreements and worker classification
- basic liability insurance for the shop

## Example plan

1. confirm licensing (shop license, each barber's individual license, and any apprentice rules if relevant)
2. set up a simple booking system so clients can see availability and book themselves
3. build a no-show/cancellation policy and put it somewhere clients actually see it
4. create a basic client record system (name, phone, what they usually get, last visit)
5. set up payment processing that also makes tipping easy
6. if renting chairs, write a clear booth-rental agreement before anyone moves in
7. get liability insurance in place
8. launch with a simple booking page and a Google Business Profile

## Example automation opportunities

- online booking with automatic confirmation and reminder texts
- automatic rebooking reminders (e.g., "it's been 4 weeks since your last cut")
- birthday or loyalty messages to regular clients
- review requests sent automatically after a visit
- waitlist notifications when an earlier slot opens up
- for booth-rental shops: automatic rent reminders/collection

## Example design direction

- clean, confident, masculine-leaning visual style (though this varies by shop identity — a modern unisex barbershop will look different from a classic barbershop)
- photos of real work (fades, beard work, the actual space) rather than stock photography
- an easy-to-find "Book Now" button, front and center
- clear pricing so clients aren't guessing

## Example AI use cases

- draft a no-show policy in plain language
- write booking confirmation and reminder text templates
- draft a simple booth-rental agreement outline to review with a lawyer
- summarize client notes into a quick "what they usually get" reference
- write social captions for before/after photos
