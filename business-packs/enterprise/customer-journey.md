# Enterprise — Internal Decision & Information Journey

The other packs in this directory map a customer's journey. At enterprise scale, the more urgent journey to fix is often internal: how a decision or a piece of information actually moves through the organization. A business that has outgrown its systems usually shows the strain right here, long before customers notice.

## 1. Something needs a decision
At a small scale, this went straight to the owner and got resolved in a conversation. At enterprise scale, it's often unclear who should even make the call — and by default, it still routes to the owner/founder, even when it shouldn't.

## 2. It reaches the wrong (or no) owner
Without clear decision rights, the issue either sits unresolved, gets escalated further than necessary, or gets decided inconsistently by whoever happens to pick it up. This is where a lot of enterprise-scale dysfunction actually starts — not with bad people, but with unclear ownership.

## 3. It reaches the founder/owner as a bottleneck
Even a well-intentioned founder becomes a genuine constraint on the business once there are too many decisions flowing through one person. Response time slows, and the founder ends up spending time on decisions that a manager could (and should) be trusted to make.

## 4. It gets decided, but the decision doesn't propagate
The decision gets made, but doesn't reliably reach everyone who needs to know it — a classic symptom of growth outpacing communication systems. Different departments or locations end up operating on outdated or inconsistent information.

## 5. It becomes tribal knowledge instead of a documented process
Even when the decision is a good one, if it isn't written down, it lives only in the memory of whoever made it. The next similar situation gets re-decided from scratch, sometimes differently, by someone else.

## 6. Leadership loses visibility
Multiply the above across every department and location, and leadership ends up without a clear, current view of how the business is actually running — often not realizing it until a real problem surfaces.

## Where to focus first

If you can only fix one stage right now, fix **stage 2 (unclear ownership)**. Clear decision rights — who owns what, and who can decide without escalating — is the single highest-leverage fix at this stage of growth. It directly reduces founder bottlenecking (stage 3), and it's the prerequisite for the process documentation (stage 5) and visibility (stage 6) that come after it.
